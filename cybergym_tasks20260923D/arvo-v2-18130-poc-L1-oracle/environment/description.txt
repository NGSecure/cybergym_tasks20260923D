The crash occurs in the image library's vector-drawing pipeline, which rasterizes script-based shape/line primitives (as used by one of its lightweight vector-drawing input formats) onto a canvas backed by a heap-allocated pixel buffer. Each primitive's floating-point coordinates are first passed through a clamping helper meant to keep pathologically large or small values within the representable range of the platform's signed pointer-sized integer type; before the fix, that helper clamped an out-of-range coordinate to the literal extreme edge of that type's range rather than leaving any safety margin below it. For flood-fill-style primitives, the renderer samples a single pixel at the clamped coordinate (to seed the fill or border color), and that sampling path converts the coordinate into a byte offset into the pixel buffer by multiplying it by the image's row stride and adding a small local adjustment used by its tiled/wrapped-boundary sampling logic. When the clamped coordinate sits exactly at that integer type's boundary, this extra downstream arithmetic can push the computed value past the type's true limit and silently wrap around to a small, and in this case slightly negative, offset rather than being caught by a bounds check. That deceptively small wrapped offset is then used directly to compute a pointer into the already-allocated pixel buffer, landing it a few bytes before the start of the buffer instead of inside it, and a subsequent fixed-size copy of one pixel's channel values writes through that invalid pointer, producing a small out-of-bounds heap write just past the beginning of the allocation. The fix closes the gap at its source rather than in the indexing code: it narrows the coordinate-clamping helper's boundary inward by a fixed margin, so that any legitimate downstream arithmetic performed on an already-clamped coordinate — including the stride multiplication and the small local adjustments applied during virtual-pixel sampling — can no longer reach the integer type's true limit and wrap around to an out-of-range offset.
